# Street Political Economy: Field Operations & Route Dynamics

**Target Application:** `waste-scan-mate` (Operator Handheld Edge Terminal)  
**Domain Focus:** Street-Level Power Dynamics, Informal Levies, and Route Friction

---

## 1. The Agbero & Transport Union Tax
- **The Street Boy Reality:** In commercial nodes like Ikotun Roundabout, Akowonjo, and Agbado Crossing, transport union youths (*Agberos*) and local street boys (*Omo Onile*) establish informal checkpoints.
- **The Shakedown:** Compactor trucks stopping to empty commercial bins or Dino skips are frequently accosted for "parking money" or "loading levy" (typically ₦500–₦1,500 per stop).
- **Mitigation:** The system integrates official vehicle clearance barcodes with Local Government revenue memorandums. Tricycle operators operate under recognized Zone Micro-Collector credentials, legally shielding them from street harassment.

## 2. Dumpsite Queue Economy & Midnight Cartels
- **The Igando Bottleneck:** The road to Solous II along the LASU-Igando expressway is a major logistics chokepoint. When tipping bays flood during heavy rains, queues stretch over 3 kilometers.
- **The Black-Market Bypass:** Informal fixers charge drivers up to ₦10,000 to "jump the queue" or illegally divert waste to unauthorized private burrow pits.
- **System Defense:** The handheld terminal monitors the live 45-minute SLA timer. When queue density at Solous II exceeds capacity, dispatch automatically reroutes trucks to Agege TLS, bypassing the extortion network.

## 3. Waste Pickers & Scavenger Syndicates
- **The 'Baba Oja' Factor:** At every major transfer point and skip, informal waste pickers sift through waste to recover metals, PET bottles, and cardboard before trucks arrive.
- **Operational Integration:** Rather than criminalizing pickers, the terminal's drop-off protocol allows registered pickers (`REC-PICK-*`) to log source-separated recyclable batches at designated sorting pads, turning potential route saboteurs into formal sorting allies.
